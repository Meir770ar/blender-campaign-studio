# Performance, contact and final polish

Use for directed human/product action and timecoded campaign feedback. These lessons come from a reviewed spec production, not a universal brand manual or model leaderboard.

## Prove the story and difficult action early

Build the relationship/action arc before finishing. Pilot the hardest necessary interaction (walking, handoff, opening, drinking) with the canonical first frame, then review a rough cut at normal speed with sound before scaling generation or rendering. If agent playback/listening is unavailable, arrange concrete user review at that point, not only after expensive finishing. Do not impose an extra approval when existing authorization already covers production; distinguish permission from missing creative evidence.

Audit the whole adjacent sequence after a correction: an upward arrival fails if another retained insert descends. Maintain a compact continuity ledger for identity, clothes, screen direction, hand ownership, product count, cap state and table positions. First-frame geometry is part of blocking: contact surfaces, reachable grips and clear occlusion must already be plausible.

## Direct observable mechanics

| Action | Direction | Evidence to inspect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stair ascent | Plant support sole; move torso over it; extend loaded knee; clear next riser with trailing foot; let carried weight lag modestly | Support foot fixed relative to tread during loading, alternating landings, body rise and cooler inertia; no skating or stone penetration |
| Handoff | Receiver wraps upper object body while giver still supports lower body; brief overlapping grip; giver releases only after receiver secures it | Continuous ownership, opposed thumb/fingers, rigid object silhouette, no unheld interval or fused fingers |
| Group sip | Assign each actor a distinct start, duration, tilt, lowering time, eyeline and reaction | Actual unequal starts/ends; early finisher reacts while others drink; each keeps own bottle |
| Opening | Specify opener engagement, leverage, release and whether cap remains held or falls | Follow cap through release to retention/set-down or a credible gravity-driven fall; no vanishing, teleportation or impossible rebound |
| Drinking | Open rim reaches lips, modest tilt, sip, separation and lowering | Rim/lower-lip boundary and facial occlusion before/during/after contact; stable glass, grip and label |

“Natural” and “slightly staggered” are insufficient blocking on their own. Give per-actor beats without treating exact example timings as a reusable fixed recipe. Do not require everyone to drink simultaneously or add identical satisfaction sighs. Check the achieved motion, not prompt compliance claims.

Use tight coverage to simplify a difficult interaction while preserving eyelines, contact and the required action. Record crop/trim compromises: excluding an extra generated bottle from the delivered composition is an editorial repair, not a corrected source asset. Check all frames of the crop, camera travel, face/hand clearance and useful resolution. Do not hide a required action or introduce a new continuity contradiction.

## Interpret the last few percent correctly

- Review critical contacts both at 1x and frame by frame. A subtle rim/lip intersection can survive a successful wide-shot review; record its time range and severity without declaring the whole film either perfect or unusable.
- A light metal-on-wood sound can support a cap landing only when staged action supports that event, including a motivated off-screen landing. It does not repair a cap that visibly vanishes. A retained cap/set-down does not require a falling arc. Inspect before adopting a reviewer's physical explanation as fact.
- Add residual fizz relative to the actual opening event, with selected source-in, internal attack, duration and gain. A half-second tail is a useful option for this case, not a default for every bottle. Do not add a second opening pop.
- Reduce a disproportionate single sigh or choose an existing natural reaction; do not automatically synthesize a choir of breaths. Generated native audio can contain unwanted speech or music and may inseparably mix ambience/Foley.
- Match adjacent exposure, black levels, saturation and skin/brand color before effects. Compare the opening and following wide shot directly; do not copy grading numbers to another generation.
- Compare a small number of licensed AAA Hebrew typography treatments only when typography is in scope. Evaluate glyph shape, weight, optical spacing, RTL punctuation, line width, hierarchy and phone-size readability. A rounded face or slightly open spacing is an artistic option, not an authenticated Coca-Cola font rule. Preserve shaping and avoid indiscriminate tracking of combining marks. Keep approved logo geometry intact.

## Evidence, restraint and handoff

Record feedback against the exact reviewed version: timecode, observation, evidence source (user 1x/listening, agent frame inspection, automated measurement), confidence, severity, preserved successful elements and proposed action. User praise of timing/Foley is actual listener feedback for that version; it does not establish sample-perfect sync, official five-note sonic identity, endorsement or broadcaster compliance. Percentages such as “97%” are subjective feedback, not measured certification.

If user asks for lessons/skill changes only, make no new commercial, paid generation or media modification. Preserve remaining micro-defects as observations and potential repairs, not completed fixes. Broader approval to produce earlier versions does not override this boundary.

Keep story/blocking → source performance → integration → graphics/color → sound → encoded QA as a repair priority, with sound planned from the beginning. Preserve successful clips, music/VO and ending unless feedback actually changes them. A native 3D insert must match neighboring footage at full delivery resolution; discard an unnatural insert rather than retain it to demonstrate Blender usage.

Provider choice remains shot-specific. Validate current catalog and exact output fps/resolution/audio metadata; do not convert one successful FLUX/WAN/MiniMax trial into a universal recommendation. Provider planners cannot replace an established brief or current authorization. Reconcile content-addressed jobs and actual cumulative usage; open budget is not a reason for blind iteration. Stills retain the user's Codex-only route.
